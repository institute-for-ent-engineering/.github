# Institute for Enterprise Infrastructure Engineering

The Institute for Enterprise Infrastructure Engineering (IEIE) was established through research into Enterprise Infrastructure Services within IT Operations. As this work progressed, it became apparent that many of the underlying engineering principles were not unique to IT infrastructure, but instead reflected more general properties of complex operational systems.  The Institute therefore distinguishes between its **method of inquiry** and its **domain of inquiry**.  The method of inquiry is an evidence-based engineering methodology founded upon scientific observation, hypothesis testing, dependency modeling, and continuous refinement.  

The Institute's initial domain of inquiry is **Enterprise Infrastructure Platform Engineering**, with particular emphasis on Enterprise Infrastructure Platform Engineering (EIPE) and the engineering of Enterprise Infrastructure Services within IT Operations.

As the body of evidence expands, the Institute expects these methods to be evaluated across additional classes of complex enterprise systems.

## Method of Inquiry

The Institute applies a common evidence-based method of inquiry across its research programs. The method is intended to produce engineering models that are explicit, testable, predictive, and subject to continuous refinement through observation.

The Institute's method of inquiry is founded on the following principles:

- **Observation precedes inference.** Conclusions must be grounded in observable evidence rather than assumptions about how a system is expected to behave.

- **Hypotheses generate predictions.** A hypothesis must produce observable predictions that can be tested against the behavior of the system under study.

- **Evidence constrains inference.** Conclusions must remain traceable to the evidence from which they are derived. Evidence that contradicts a model must not be discarded merely because it is inconsistent with the expected result.

- **Systems are analyzed through relationships and dependencies.** Components cannot be understood solely in isolation. Their interactions, dependencies, constraints, and effects on system behavior must also be examined.

- **Models must be testable.** Engineering models must make predictions about system behavior that can be compared with observed outcomes.

- **Interventions provide evidence.** Changes introduced into a system provide an opportunity to test predicted effects against observed effects.

- **Differences between predicted and observed outcomes refine the model.** A discrepancy is evidence that the model may be incomplete or incorrect and must be investigated rather than explained away.

- **Models remain provisional.** Confidence in a model may increase through repeated validation, but models remain subject to revision when new evidence warrants it.

- **Human behavior is part of the system.** Organizational structures, incentives, decision making, communication, institutional knowledge, and other human factors must be considered when they materially affect system behavior.

- **Technology is an enabling mechanism.** Technology executes or supports an engineered model; it does not define the intent or methodology of the system being engineered.

- **Artificial Intelligence is a computational tool.** AI may assist observation, analysis, inference, modeling, validation, and execution, but it does not replace the evidence, reasoning, or engineering methodology upon which conclusions are based.

## Active Research Projects ##
The Institute pursues its mission through focused research projects that apply the Institute's Method of Inquiry to specific areas of enterprise infrastructure engineering. Each project establishes its own research questions, hypotheses, domain of inquiry, and engineering artifacts while contributing to the Institute's broader body of knowledge.
