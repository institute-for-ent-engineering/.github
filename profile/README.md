# Institute for Enterprise Infrastructure Engineering

The **Institute for Enterprise Infrastructure Engineering (IEIE)** was established through research into Enterprise Infrastructure Services within IT Operations. As this work progressed, we began to identify underlying principles that did not appear to be unique to IT infrastructure, but instead reflected more general properties of complex operational systems. This observation led us to distinguish between the **Method of Inquiry** (MOI) and the **Domain of Inquiry** (DOI).  Our method of inquiry is evidence-based, founded upon scientific observation, hypothesis testing, dependency modeling, and continuous refinement.  As the body of evidence expands, the Institute expects these methods to be evaluated across additional classes of complex enterprise systems.

---

## Method of Inquiry

The Method of Inquiry (MOI) defines how the Institute approaches a problem.  We observe operational systems, develop explicit models and hypotheses, derive predictions from those models, and compare predicted outcomes with what is actually observed. Differences between prediction and observation provide evidence for refinement of the model.  The Institute may draw upon methods and principles from any established discipline that contributes to understanding the system under study. 

---

## Domain of Inquiry ##
A Domain of Inquiry (DOI) defines where the Institute applies its Method of Inquiry. The Institute may explore any domain in which its methods appear applicable to understanding complex operational systems.

---

## Active Domains of Inquiry ##
The Institute applies its **Method of Inquiry** through active **Domains of Inquiry**. Each domain establishes the boundaries within which questions are examined, models are developed, and evidence is collected and evaluated.


### Enterprise Infrastructure Platform Engineering ###
Examines how existing infrastructure capabilities can be refactored and engineered as explicit enterprise services whose operational behavior can be understood, governed, automated, measured, and predicted.<br>
